October 2026 · Quantum error correction

Improved zero-dimensional qudit stabilizer codes of odd length from circulant graphs

Four circulant graph codes, $\code{19}{0}{9}{5}$, $\code{25}{0}{11}{5}$, $\code{29}{0}{12}{5}$ and $\code{21}{0}{10}{7}$, each raise the best known lower bound in Grassl’s qudit code tables by one. They were found by an autonomous multi-agent language-model search working against a frozen, test-validated verifier, and every minimum distance is certified exactly.

October 2026 · Quantum circuits

Certified low-T-count multipliers for binary fields from Chinese remaindering

Ancilla-free Clifford+T circuits for multiplication in GF($2^n$) for thirteen field sizes, $n = 5$–8, 10–16, 32 and 64, released as explicit gate lists with a standalone checker. Where a published decomposition with correct released data exists, they use fewer T gates, for example 113 against 123 at $n = 8$ and 151 against 157 at $n = 10$. For $n \ge 11$ the only reported values come from decompositions that, as the paper shows, do not decompose GF($2^n$) multiplication. The circuits come from one composition principle: if the product can be computed by Chinese remaindering in small algebras $A_i$ (subfields, local rings or the points of an algebraic curve), the T-count is at most $\sum_i \mathcal{T}(A_i)$. Proof objects and checker.

October 2026 · Quantum nonlocality

Certified Tsirelson bounds for 229 tight bipartite Bell inequalities

The maximal quantum violations of the tight bipartite Bell inequalities with up to five binary settings per party were known only from floating-point computations. This paper certifies windows for 229 of the 239 catalogued inequalities, each pairing an exact quantum strategy with an exact sum-of-squares certificate, and 223 of them are narrower than $10^{-7}$. It closes $J^{97}_{4422}$ to within $2.2\times10^{-8}$, closes three more rigorously, and finds that the published maximum of $A_{19}$ is slightly too low.

October 2026 · Quantum nonlocality

An improved upper bound on the Grothendieck constant of order three from symmetric local models

The Grothendieck constant $K_G(3)$ is the inverse of the critical visibility below which the two-qubit Werner state has a local hidden-variable model for all projective measurements. This paper proves $K_G(3)\le 1.44942$, improving the bound 1.4546 of Designolle et al. (2023) and shrinking the known window for $K_G(3)$ by 29%. The proof is an explicit local model for 721 measurement directions, extended to all directions by a shrinking argument and verified in exact rational arithmetic. The certificate is released with a standalone exact checker that shares no code with the project verifier. Proof objects and checker.

October 2026 · Quantum entanglement

Absolutely maximally entangled states with local dimensions 8 and 16, a coupling rule for composite dimensions, and a confirmation that no stabilizer AME(8,4) state exists

An $n$-party state is absolutely maximally entangled, $\mathrm{AME}(n,d)$, if every set of $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ parties is maximally mixed. This paper constructs $\mathrm{AME}(15,8)$ and $\mathrm{AME}(17,8)$, two cases that were open, and, beyond the table of known states, $\mathrm{AME}(n,16)$ for $n = 18$–21. A coupling rule combines a $k$-uniform state of dimension $a$ with an $\mathrm{AME}(2k+2,b)$ state into an $\mathrm{AME}(2k+1,ab)$ state and gives 26 further cells with composite $d \le 62$. All 32 states are certified exactly. For $\mathrm{AME}(8,4)$ it confirms, with an independent search, Ball, Moreno and Simoens's proof that no stabilizer state exists.

October 2026 · Quantum channels

Certified thresholds for positive quantum capacity of the depolarizing, independent X–Z and two-Pauli channels

Explicit rank-two permutation-symmetric inputs on up to 480 qubits prove that the depolarizing channel has positive quantum capacity up to $p = 0.066882$ per Pauli, a total error of 20.06%, above the certified value 0.064956 of Krohn-Grimberghe (2026). For the independent X–Z and two-Pauli channels, where only numerical thresholds near 0.118 were known, they certify $Q > 0$ up to $p = 0.127705$ and at $p = 0.126774$. A frozen interval-arithmetic verifier certifies the board records, and an independent 128-bit ball-arithmetic evaluator certifies the larger values beyond the verifier's size limits. Certificates and checkers.
